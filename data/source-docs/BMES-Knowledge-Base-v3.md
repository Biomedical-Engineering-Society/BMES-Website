## 1. High-Level Overview

- Club Name: Biomedical Engineering Society – Toronto Metropolitan University Chapter
- Abbreviation: BMES TMU
- School: Toronto Metropolitan University (TMU)
- Faculty: Faculty of Engineering & Architectural Science (FEAS)
- Home Department: Electrical, Computer and Biomedical Engineering (ECBE)
- Parent Student Society: Metropolitan Undergraduate Engineering Society (MUES)
- International Parent Organization: Biomedical Engineering Society (BMES)

BMES TMU is a student-run chapter of the international Biomedical Engineering Society, which serves as the leading professional society for biomedical engineers and bioengineers, with more than 6,000 members across North America (BMES.org, 2026). As the TMU chapter, BMES supports biomedical engineering and related students through events, professional development, hands-on opportunities, career development, skill-building, mentorship, and wellness initiatives.

Updated (v3): membership figures for the international parent organization reflect BMES.org's current public messaging (6,000+ professionals). BMES TMU's chapter branding now uses an updated logo, referenced on the MUES chapter directory page.

## 2. Mission, Vision & Core Pillars

### 2.1 Mission

BMES TMU's mission is to support and empower biomedical engineering students at TMU by promoting:

- Community & belonging
- Career development & professional growth
- Mentorship & networking
- Wellness & mental health
- Technical exploration & innovation

This is delivered through accessible programming — events, workshops, conferences, and outreach activities — that reflect the needs and values of the student body.

### 2.2 Vision

BMES TMU aims to become the leading student hub for biomedical innovation, mentorship, and industry engagement at TMU, and an active participant in National BMES activities. Chapter goals include:

- Expanding formal mentorship programs (undergrad ↔ grad ↔ alumni ↔ faculty)
- Deepening relationships with companies in med-tech, biotech, and health-tech
- Growing its flagship conference and Alumni Night
- Increasing hands-on workshops, lab tours, and design collaborations
- Eventually attending and contributing to the National BMES Meeting

### 2.3 Core Pillars

BMES programming and identity map to four core pillars — use these to classify any event or answer “what kind of things does BMES do?”:

- Community & Wellness — social events, destressor events, care packages, game nights, pub nights.
- Career Development & Mentorship — alumni panels, resume feedback, networking events, mentorship initiatives, CPR certification, career workshops.
- Technical Innovation & Academics — design competitions, case competitions, the flagship conference, technical workshops, lab tours, collaborations with design teams and researchers.
- Outreach & Societal Impact — fundraising, health awareness campaigns, open house tabling, frosh engagement.

## 3. Who We Serve

- Undergraduate biomedical engineering students at TMU
- Students from other engineering/science programs interested in health-tech, medical devices, or biomedical innovation
- Occasionally, prospective students and first-years through open houses and frosh events

Membership is open — students do not need to be national BMES members to attend most events. The chapter also maintains a subset of National BMES members alongside its general membership.

## 4. Organizational Structure

### 4.1 Executive Roles

The chapter's executive structure (per the BMES TMU constitution) includes:

- President — overall leadership, strategy, external representation, coordination with the faculty advisor and BMES HQ.
- Vice President — supports the president, maintains team cohesion, helps oversee portfolios.
- VP Events — plans and executes events (academic, social, wellness), books spaces, coordinates logistics and catering. May be shared by two people.
- VP Finance — budgeting, expenses, reimbursement, financial reporting.
- VP Marketing — branding, social media (Instagram @bmes.tmu, LinkedIn), graphics, posters, outreach campaigns.
- VP Communications — bookings, email communications, information distribution to members.
- VP Operations — inventory, logistical support, supplies, operational planning for major events (e.g., case competitions).
- Directors & Representatives — support each VP portfolio and run specific initiatives; includes a first-year representative and website developer/manager roles.

The chapter also maintains a Faculty Advisor who liaises with the department and National BMES.

## 5. What BMES Does — Event Types & Examples

### 5.1 Flagship & Professional Events

#### BMES Synapse Conference (formerly “Engineering the Future of Health”)

Updated (v3): the chapter's flagship annual conference has been rebranded as the BMES Synapse Conference. The 2026 edition ran Saturday, February 14, 2026, 10:30 AM–6:00 PM, at the Daphne Cockwell Complex (DCC) on TMU campus.

- Concept: “Synapse” — the junction that lets the nervous system communicate — used as a metaphor for the conference as a hub connecting the biomedical engineering community at the intersection of biological signals and digital solutions.
- Format: full-day conference with keynote speaker(s), technical workshops, a case/design competition, and an industry panel.
- Perks: complimentary lunch and dinner (with dietary restrictions accommodated) and free LinkedIn headshots for attendees.
- Prior (2025) edition theme: “Engineering the Future of Health” — keynote on hard tissue engineering/biomaterials/biomineralization, a workshop on embolic biomaterials for vascular embolotherapy, an entrepreneurship talk from student startup GlucoSense, an in-house prosthetics case competition, and an industry panel (e.g., Conavi Medical, Ontario Genomics, public health, med-device companies). Attendance was roughly 200 students overall (85+ in core programming).

Exact theme, speakers, and attendance figures shift year to year. When asked about “this year's conference,” the bot should give the confirmed Synapse branding/date if within the relevant window and otherwise defer to Instagram/website for the current year's specifics.

#### Alumni Night & Resume Roast

Networking event bringing TMU alumni and industry professionals to campus. Students receive resume feedback and hear about real-world career paths (med-device, software, consulting, and related fields). Historical attendance: 75+ students.

#### CPR C Certification Workshop

Run with SAJE Vital Signs; students earn CPR C certification at a discounted rate, focused on practical, life-saving skills relevant to clinical and workplace settings.

### 5.2 Wellness & Community Events

#### Rest & Reset (Destressor)

Held near final exams. Activities have included gingerbread house competitions, bracelet making, origami, a photobooth, and a social media contest, with 100+ care packages handed out. Focus: mental health, peer connection, a break from academics.

#### Board Games & Trivia Night

Early-semester social night with games, trivia, snacks, and small prizes — aimed at building early-year community and lowering the barrier to joining BMES.

#### Movember Pub & Karaoke Night

Co-hosted with other student groups; a fundraiser and awareness event for men's mental and physical health under the Movember campaign, with karaoke, games, and matched donations.

#### BMES Design Competition / Design-A-Thon

A hands-on design-focused competition where student teams tackle a real-world biomedical engineering challenge from concept to prototype pitch (recent theme: prosthetics innovation). Teams develop problem statements rooted in clinical/rehab needs, propose device mechanisms and biomedical rationale, consider materials and manufacturability, and pitch high-level implementation plans. Judged by BMES executives and upper-year BME students on creativity, feasibility, usability, clinical impact, and presentation clarity. Serves as an accessible entry point into biomedical design thinking and preparation for the conference's case competition.

These events reflect that BMES is not only about professional development, but also community, charity, and outreach.

## 7. Governance & Constitution (Short Version for the Bot)

- BMES TMU is an official BMES Student Chapter, chartered by the international Biomedical Engineering Society.
- Membership is open to students enrolled in eligible programs who are in good standing; national BMES membership is encouraged but not required to participate.
- Core officer positions: President, VP, VP Events, VP Finance, VP Marketing, VP Communications, VP Operations.
- President & VP are chosen via interviews conducted by the outgoing President & VP; other officers are elected by members; Directors are chosen via interview.
- Officers must be in good academic standing and not about to leave on internship before serving at least half their term.
- A Faculty Advisor (a BMES member) is appointed annually.
- Constitution/bylaw amendments require a 3/4 vote and must also be sent to MUES.

The bot doesn't need every procedural detail but should be able to answer basic governance questions like “how are execs chosen?” and “do you have a constitution?”

## 10. Suggested Chatbot Responses (Expanded)

### 10.1 “What is BMES?” / “Tell me about your club”

BMES TMU is the Biomedical Engineering Society chapter at Toronto Metropolitan University. We're a student-run club that supports biomedical engineering and health-tech students through community events, networking, mentorship, wellness programming, and technical opportunities like our flagship conference and case competitions. Our goal is to help students grow academically, professionally, and personally while building a strong, inclusive community.

### 10.2 “What do you do?” / “What kinds of events do you run?”

We focus on four main pillars:

- Community & Wellness: game nights, the Rest & Reset destressor, care-package events, Movember pub & karaoke night, etc.
- Career & Mentorship: Alumni Night & Resume Roast, career panels, CPR certification, networking with industry and alumni.
- Technical & Innovation: the BMES Synapse Conference, our Design Competition/Design-A-Thon, workshops led by researchers and startups.
- Outreach & Impact: charity fundraisers, patch sales for disability resources, open house and frosh expo tabling.

Each semester mixes social, academic, and professional events so there's something for everyone.

### 10.3 “How do I join?” / “How can I get involved?”

BMES is open to all TMU students — you don't need to be in BME to start coming out! The easiest ways to get involved:

- Attend our events (watch Instagram @bmes.tmu or our chapter page for dates).
- Say hi at our outreach tables during Frosh, Open House, or other faculty events.
- Apply for volunteer, director, or executive roles when applications open each year.

You can also email bmes@mues.ca to ask about current opportunities.

### 10.4 “Do I have to be in Biomedical Engineering?” / “Can other programs join?”

You do not have to be a BME major to participate. While many members are biomedical engineering students, BMES welcomes anyone interested in health-tech, medical devices, or biomedical innovation — including students from other engineering and science programs.

### 10.5 “What is the BMES Synapse Conference?”

The BMES Synapse Conference is our flagship annual conference — the successor to our previous “Engineering the Future of Health” conference. It's a full-day event with a keynote talk, technical workshops, a case/design competition, and an industry panel and networking session, plus perks like complimentary meals and free LinkedIn headshots. It's designed to help students explore career paths, learn about current research, and connect with professionals and peers. Exact dates, themes, and speakers change year to year, so check @bmes.tmu or bmes@mues.ca for the current edition.

### 10.6 “What is Rest & Reset?” / “What do you do for wellness?”

Rest & Reset is our big end-of-semester destressor event. We hand out exam care packages and run relaxing activities like gingerbread house building, bracelet making, creative crafts, and photo booths — a chance to unwind with friends, de-stress before exams, and recharge in a supportive environment. We also run other wellness-focused events and collaborations throughout the year that prioritize mental health and community.

### 10.7 “What is Alumni Night & Resume Roast?”

Alumni Night & Resume Roast is a professional development event where TMU alumni and industry professionals come back to campus to share their career journeys in biomedical and related fields and give students personalized resume feedback — a great way to learn about different career paths, get honest advice, and build your network in a low-pressure environment.

### 10.8 “How is the club structured?” / “Who leads BMES?”

BMES TMU is led by a student executive team that includes a President, Vice President, and VPs of Events, Finance, Marketing, Communications, and Operations, plus supporting directors and volunteers. We also have a Faculty Advisor from the Department of Biomedical Engineering who supports the chapter and connects us with the wider academic and BMES community. For the current names on the exec team, check @bmes.tmu on Instagram or email bmes@mues.ca — the roster changes each year.

### 10.9 “Can I become an executive / director?”

Yes! We recruit new executives and directors every year. Executive elections and applications typically happen near the end of the Winter term; director and committee roles are usually selected through applications and interviews. Exact dates and details change each year, so the best way to stay updated is to follow @bmes.tmu on Instagram or check your TMU email for announcements.

### 10.10 “How do I contact you?”

You can reach us at: Email — bmes@mues.ca; Instagram — @bmes.tmu; LinkedIn — Biomedical Engineering Society (BMES TMU). For most questions about events, volunteering, or collaborations, Instagram DMs and email are the best places to start.

### 10.11 “Do you work with industry or other organizations?”

Yes. BMES regularly collaborates with TMU departments and services (e.g., the Department of Biomedical Engineering, the Career & Co-op Centre), external medical device/health-tech companies who join us for panels, workshops, and our conference, and charitable/health-focused organizations through fundraisers and awareness initiatives. If you're interested in partnering with us, email bmes@mues.ca with some details about your idea.

### 10.12 “What are your long-term goals?”

Our long-term goals include growing our mentorship programs between undergrads, grads, alumni, and faculty; strengthening industry partnerships to support more panels, case competitions, and sponsored projects; continuing to build out the BMES Synapse Conference as a well-known annual hub for biomedical innovation at TMU; and eventually enabling our chapter to attend and present at National BMES Meetings. Overall, we want BMES TMU to be the place where students feel supported, connected, and inspired to build the future of healthcare and biomedical engineering.
